# What makes a lot of information inspiring and easy to digest (onboarding, mission-driven orgs)

Quick research pass, Nov 23, for the US Ops playbook redo. Mostly practitioner sources (handbook roundups, nonprofit onboarding guides, UX writing on progressive disclosure), not formal studies.

## Principles applied

1. **Why before how.** Open with the mission and the dream, then roles, then rules. Volunteers stay for the higher purpose, not the task list. (Idealist, Qgiv, Donorbox)
2. **One idea per screen.** Netflix's culture deck keeps each slide to a sentence or two; the playbook gives each step one headline and 3-5 short lines. (Netflix deck via AirMason / CaseIQ)
3. **Progressive disclosure.** Show the essentials first, reveal detail on demand (steps, role drill-downs), never everything at once. (LogRocket, Pendo, uxdesign.cc)
4. **Chunk it.** Short, scannable groups beat long lists; break complex processes into sequential steps. (LogRocket, DEV Community)
5. **Talk like a person.** Conversational, plain language; Valve and Basecamp handbooks are praised for reading like a human wrote them. (BambooHR on Valve, Process Street on Basecamp, Fast Company)
6. **Make the purpose concrete.** A hero story and real numbers make the mission tangible; show how each person's work connects to it. (Qgiv, Idealist, Niche Academy)
7. **Show what good looks like in practice.** Concrete examples and clear expectations build confidence and belonging, the two things tied to volunteer retention. (Niche Academy, Litmos)
8. **Start from the reader's role.** People look for "what's mine"; a role-first entry point gets them to their own expectations fast. (Litmos: clear expectations and placement)
9. **End with a clear next action.** Every section closes with what to do or who to go to.
